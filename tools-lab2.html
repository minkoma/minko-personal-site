Tools, without the boxes

same real logos, five ways to hold them

Every option below drops the tray, tile and card entirely: the marks sit straight on the paper. They are ordered loosely from "says something about how you work" to "quietest".

option 1

The edit timeline

Your own craft as the container. One track per group, each tool a clip on it, and a playhead sweeping the whole thing. It reads as a working session rather than a skills list, and a longer group just means a longer track.

option 2

One sentence

No layout at all: the logos are set inline in a handwritten sentence, at text size, so they read as words. The most personal of the five and the only one that says what you do with each tool rather than just that you own it.

option 3

Lassoed on the desk

The logos are scattered loose, and each group is circled with a hand-drawn line that draws itself in. Keeps the scrapbook feel of the trays with nothing rectangular anywhere. Hover a mark for its name.

option 4

Drifting rows

One slow row per group, alternating direction, pausing on hover. Takes the least vertical space of anything here and never grows taller as tools are added. The trade: a given logo is not always on screen.

option 5

The pipeline

Groups replaced by the stages of a real campaign, so the section explains a process instead of an inventory. The mapping below is my guess — tell me where each tool actually belongs in how you work and I'll fix it.

round two · every name spelled out, tidier

Three with full names

What you liked about the timeline is that nothing is a guessing game: the logo and the words sit together. These three keep that and drop the movement, so the block reads as a list you can scan rather than a scene.

option 6

Two columns, ruled

The quietest version. Two columns of groups, one tool per line, logo then name, a hairline between rows. Nothing moves, nothing overlaps, and the whole toolkit is readable in one pass. Hover nudges a row and brightens it.

option 7

One line per group

Each group gets a single row: the handwritten label on the left, then its tools in a line with full names. The most compact of the name-bearing options, and the left edge of every row lines up, so it reads like a contents page.

Interactive: hover a tool and it lifts with an underline in its own brand colour while the rest recede. Click it to open a line about what you use it for, under its own row — click again or press Escape to close. Click a group label to solo that row and fade the others; the label also shows how many tools are in it. Works on tap and on keyboard. The one-liners are my draft words — edit or delete each one.

option 8

The timeline, snapped to a grid

Option 1 with the mess taken out: every clip sits in one cell of a shared six-column grid, so the clip edges line up in straight vertical lines all the way down and no playhead sweeps across. Still a timeline, now ruled.
