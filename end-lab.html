How the page should end

a better last frame than "Say hi!" + an email

Two separate questions here. First, what the headline says. Second, what the visitor actually does, because right now the only move offered is "write me an email from scratch", which is the highest-effort thing you can ask of someone.

first, the words

Instead of "Say hi!"

Grouped by what they ask for. The right one depends on who you want landing here: a recruiter reading top to bottom, or a brand wondering if you take work.

  • Let's make somethinginvites work, not conversation
  • What should we make?same, as a question to them
  • Got a story to tell?ties the ending to your own word, storyteller
  • Your turnshort, after a whole page of you talking
  • I'd love to hear yoursreads as a reply to "Bits of me"
  • Roll creditsfilm ending; pairs with option 1 below
  • That's me, so farhonest, open-ended, very you
  • Still scrolling? Let's talkplayful, rewards the reader
  • Come find mewarm without being a form
  • Open to good projectsstates availability outright
  • Next scene?shortest, most film-literate
  • Hi back?keeps your voice, flips the direction

my pick: Your turn as the headline, with “open to good projects” as the small line under it. One sets the tone, the other does the job.

option 1

Closing credits

The page ends the way your films do: a slow credit roll that names what you did on the site itself, then the contact lines as the last card. Hovering pauses the roll. Highest personality, and nobody scrolls past a credit sequence.

option 7

The guestbook

The visitor draws something, signs it, and it pins to the wall beside the pad. The most fun of anything here and the only one that gives them something to do rather than something to click. Honest limit: with no backend the notes live only in that visitor's browser and vanish on reload, and the drawing can't ride along in an email. To keep them you'd need somewhere to store them — a hosted widget like guestbook.cv, or a small database. The "send it to me" button emails the typed note, not the doodle.

option 8

Scratch to reveal

The email is hidden under a foil panel; drag across it and it rubs away, revealing the address underneath. Tactile, instantly understood, and it makes the address feel like a small prize rather than a line of text. Works with a finger on a phone.

option 9

Academy leader

The countdown that opens a film reel: 3, 2, 1, then the contact card. It plays once when the section scrolls into view, and clicking it replays. The most on-brand ending for someone whose work is film, and it buys you three seconds of attention at the exact moment people usually leave.

option 10

A conversation, already started

The contact form as a chat thread: you speak first, the visitor picks a reply, and two taps later the email is composed for them. Same job as option 2 but it feels like talking to a person instead of filling in a field. Keep Gardening does this on their contact page.

option 11

Now, with a live clock

A "now" panel with one honest moving part: the real time where you are, ticking, and whether that means you're probably awake. It tells a recruiter when to expect a reply without you promising anything, and the clock makes the page feel alive and tended rather than published once and left.

option 12

The last few frames

A piece of film you drag sideways. Each frame is one line about working with you, and the final frame is THE END with the contact buttons on it. It turns the ending into a small piece of the craft, and it is the only option that can carry a sentence or two of persuasion without looking like a paragraph.
